Eight implications

  1. TIDIP. The platform launched by the Ministry of Industry and Technology, the EBRD, the World Bank Group and the European Commission ties the transformation of heavy industry to finance, with a target of EUR 5 billion+ in investment (verified). The real test is the speed of project preparation.
  2. The Antalya Green Industrialization Accord. The framework that carries Antalya's name could create new opportunities for Turkish industrialists through tools such as finance, market creation and green public procurement.
  3. The 2030 ETS allocation calendar. According to sector representatives, what actually slows investment is not finance but the uncertainty of the calendar. Clarity would speed up investment decisions.
  4. CBAM. The expansion of its scope and the verification of facility-level emission benchmarks directly affect exporting firms.
  5. The 2040 energy targets. 175 GW of wind and solar, 140 GWh of storage, 10 GW of nuclear (verified). The bottleneck in front of them is the grid and permitting times.
  6. Zero waste. The rise of the recovery rate from 13% to 37.53% (verified) puts Türkiye in the position of a global example; now it is the turn of NDC alignment and the neighbourhood scale.
  7. The Hormuz lesson. Energy security has become a matter of national sovereignty; domestic generation, storage and demand flexibility are parts of the same package of solutions.
  8. OTS and D-8. Climate cooperation gets stronger when regional diplomacy rests on concrete topics such as water, glaciers and joint satellite monitoring.

12 recommendations

  1. Announce the 2030 ETS allocation calendar and the CBAM steps early and clearly (0–6 months)
  2. Plan grid connection and permitting times together with the 2040 targets (6–18 months)
  3. Open TIDIP project preparation support to SME suppliers, OIZs and young ventures (6–18 months)
  4. Integrate weather forecasting into grid operation and demand planning (6–18 months)
  5. Build national MRV capacity that verifies facility emissions with ground and satellite observations (6–18 months)
  6. Build a physical-risk data bridge between banks, MGM and universities (6–18 months)
  7. Produce sea-level projections that include land subsidence for coastal cities (6–18 months)
  8. Combine prevention, community training and early warning in forest fires (6–18 months)
  9. Combine air quality forecasting with meteorology and present it in language the public understands (6–18 months)
  10. Take weather–climate literacy into schools and green skills into education policy (6–18 months)
  11. Publish a traceable roadmap to COP32; build a shared climate information infrastructure for the OTS and D-8 (0–6 months)
  12. Take Zero Waste to the neighbourhood scale and to young people (18+ months)
