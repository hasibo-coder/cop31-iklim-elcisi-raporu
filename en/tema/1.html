Climate Finance and Capital

The capital exists; what is missing is the bridge that connects it to long-term, predictable and investment-ready projects. This section collects the shared message I heard in the finance sessions (K01, K16, K23, K33; and secondarily K18 and K24).

Read this section in the report

What did I hear in the field?

  • Banking now works with climate. According to Garanti BBVA representatives, sustainable finance has spread from energy to heavy industry such as steel, cement and fertilizer. A company applying for credit is expected to provide climate data and a science-based decarbonization plan.
  • The real obstacle is not money but maturity and uncertainty. High inflation and commercial maturities of 2–3 years make long-horizon investment difficult. Blended finance with development banks and long-term loans with grace periods came forward as solutions.
  • Climate risk is a systemic financial risk. At the asset owners' roundtable, risk was described as non-linear, cumulative and impossible to reduce through diversification. Scenarios were said to underprice tipping points. What slows flows to emerging markets, in turn, is the perceived risk premium.
  • Capital is ready; the project pipeline is not ready enough. The Unlocking Capital session made three proposals: project preparation, recording climate action in institutional accounting, and open-source climate data.
  • Public money draws private capital into risky areas. In the Brazil Climate Fund and KfW example, blended finance mobilized private capital for risky work such as forest restoration. In Türkiye the same logic works in TIDIP (EBRD, the World Bank and the Turkish government); I cover it under Theme 3.

Through the eyes of climate science and meteorological engineering

Banks are asking for "climate data"; whether this data is reliable depends on the observation–model–forecast triad behind it. Physical risks such as drought, flood, extreme heat and storms change over the life of a loan. Historical averages are therefore not enough; forward-looking, high-resolution climate projections are needed. That scenarios underprice tipping points is a warning that should be taken seriously from a scientific point of view as well.

My recommendations as a climate ambassador

  1. Build a shared data bridge for physical risk. Set out an open methodology for the climate data banks use in credit assessment. Universities with meteorology and climate departments (such as ITU) should take a validating role.
  2. Give project preparation support to young people and early-stage ventures. The capital exists, but there are few ready projects. For ventures such as Uray, which does weather-aware route optimization, showing measurable fuel and emission savings could be the first step to accessing finance.
  3. Solve the maturity mismatch for small investors too. Loan products with grace periods and 6–10 year maturities should be supported by capital market instruments open not only to large industry but also to SMEs and small investors.
  4. Test the return on adaptation investment with Türkiye data. The claim of "about USD 10 returned for every USD 1 of adaptation investment" is speaker-reported and its source could not be verified. An independent Türkiye study should test this claim.
  5. Expand open facility-level and climate data. Open facility-level data like Climate TRACE should be made available to banks and researchers with the same transparency for facilities in Türkiye.
