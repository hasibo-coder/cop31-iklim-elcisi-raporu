Science, Diplomacy and Ministerial Sessions

In the negotiation rooms the shared language is science; in Antalya this language turned into regional cooperation and bridges to implementation. This section brings together four sessions (K05, K06, K15, K19).

Read this section in the report

What did I hear in the field?

  • The D-8 ministerial session, from Istanbul to Antalya. The session, attended by COP31 President Murat Kurum, aimed to turn the 17 July 2026 Istanbul Declaration into action. The Antalya Ministerial Action Framework, the 2027–2030 D-8 Climate and Environment Roadmap and a D-8–specific Zero Waste Roadmap came onto the agenda. The Climate Implementation Bridge matches national priorities with projects and finance without imposing targets. The Presidency also proposed a humanitarian "COP31 Antalya Gaza Climate Line" (water purification, solar-battery units, shelter).
  • Culture and indigenous knowledge enter adaptation plans. At the meeting of the Group of Friends of Culture-Based Climate Action, including cultural heritage and indigenous knowledge in National Adaptation Plans and climate finance was framed as a move "from recognition to integration". The Global Goal on Adaptation includes five cultural heritage indicators.
  • The Turkic states see water and mountain risk as shared. At the OTS Environment and Climate Ministers roundtable, the Aral Sea, water scarcity and glacier melt were presented as shared regional threats. The priority is to turn national climate plans into bankable projects; the 2027 "Year of Nature and Environmental Protection", a Central Asia climate centre in Uzbekistan and a Global Mountain Summit in Kyrgyzstan are the calendar for this.
  • Science: overshoot must not become the new normal. In the session run jointly by Australia and the Presidency, IPCC representatives, Pacific small island states and least developed countries argued for keeping the duration and magnitude of any overshoot of 1.5 °C low. It was said that coral reefs cannot be protected above 1.5 °C and that the Mitigation Work Programme must continue to 2031 with concrete indicators.

Through the eyes of climate science and meteorological engineering

The 1.5 °C limit is defined not by the temperature of a single year but by a long-term average; natural fluctuations such as El Niño can push single-year values above the limit. This distinction matters when explaining it to the public: exceeding it in one year does not mean the limit has been crossed, but the trend is a serious warning. In the session it was stressed that the return after an overshoot may not be symmetric and that systems such as coral reefs may suffer permanent damage; scientifically, this is a warning against the assumption that "we can return after the limit".

My recommendations as a climate ambassador

  1. Publish a simple, traceable roadmap from the COP31 outcomes to COP32. The Antalya Accord, the D-8 roadmap and the Zero Waste initiatives should be shared publicly on a single page with indicators and dates.
  2. Build a shared climate information infrastructure for the OTS and D-8. Joint satellite monitoring, a glacier and water observation network, early warning and data sharing, with the participation of MGM and university meteorology departments, could respond to regional risks such as the Aral Sea.
  3. Turn national climate plans into bankable projects. The spirit of the Climate Implementation Bridge should be combined, in Türkiye too, with a project preparation unit (Themes 1 and 3).
  4. Make Turkish scientists and young researchers more visible in the science–policy interface. Contributions from Türkiye as authors and reviewers to the climate services and regional dynamics chapters of the IPCC AR7 process mentioned in the session should increase; observer and internship pathways should be opened for students.
  5. Bring cultural heritage into adaptation plans with indicators. Türkiye's digitization experience and the examples of solar-powered archaeological sites could be carried into National Adaptation Plan indicators.
